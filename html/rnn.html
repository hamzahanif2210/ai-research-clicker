Networks with memory

Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) feed their own previous output back in as part of the next input, giving them a form of memory well suited to sequences — text, speech, time series.

Solving the vanishing gradient with LSTMs

Plain RNNs struggle to remember information over long sequences because gradients shrink to nothing as they propagate back in time. In 1997, Hochreiter and Schmidhuber introduced the Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) cell, with gates that let the network learn what to remember and what to forget — enabling it to model much longer-range dependencies.
